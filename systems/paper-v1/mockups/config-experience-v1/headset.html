Anchored panel — headset

Target: Headset · comfortable FOV · 1280×800 · State 2 of 3 · Status: review · Updated 2026-09-17

Intent

The same held state in a headset. The panel does the same job as the tablet's, but the constraints swap: there is no screen edge to justify against, so the panel is anchored in the room instead — about a metre out, slightly below the eye line, and it stays where the room is when you turn your head.

Why it is built this way

  • Never head-locked. A panel welded to the view cannot be looked away from, and that is what makes people take a headset off.
  • Inside the comfort band — within ±30° of the eye line, biased below centre, because looking down is cheaper than looking up.
  • Every control is --hit-xr: gaze and ray targeting need a bigger floor than a fingertip, so the touch minimum does not apply here.
  • Body type steps up a size. 17 pt at arm's length and 21 pt at a metre are the same apparent size, and the headset is the metre case.
  • Opaque panel with the heavy edge, same as the tablet: it is composited over a live room and has to hold its own contrast.

Open questions

  • Should the panel re-anchor if the wearer walks away from it, or wait to be recalled? Shown as staying put.
  • Is a two-button footer enough at this size, or does "start" want to be the only thing in it?
