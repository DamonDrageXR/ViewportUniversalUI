One-thumb strip — phone

Target: Phone · 393×852 · State 3 of 3 · Status: review · Updated 2026-09-17

Intent

The running experience on the device someone has in their pocket rather than the one they were issued. A phone held one-handed has a single reach zone — the bottom third — so the tablet's two corner arcs collapse into one strip, and the tool set is cut to what can be driven by one thumb.

Why it is built this way

  • One strip at the bottom rather than two in the corners. A phone is not a small tablet: there is no second thumb to put anything under.
  • Status sits at the top, deliberately out of reach. It is read, never pressed, so it costs the view nothing to put it there.
  • The scrubber keeps its arrows at both ends, and here they are the primary control — one thumb cannot swipe and hold at the same time.
  • Fewer tools than the tablet. The answer to a smaller reach zone is fewer controls, not smaller ones.

Open questions

  • Two stacked capsules at the bottom is close to the strip budget. Does the scrubber appear only when a timeline layer is on?
  • Is "leave the room" safe to put in the thumb strip, or does it want a confirm?
