Reach zones — iPad landscape

Target: iPad Pro · 1366×1024 · State 3 of 3 · Status: review · Updated 2026-09-17

Intent

The experience is running, which makes the view the product and the interface a visitor. Everything collapses to thin strips around the edge, placed where the thumbs of a held tablet actually are — and the middle of the screen is left alone, because it is both the thing being looked at and the hardest place on the device to reach.

Why it is built this way

  • Strips of 96 px or less, so the view keeps at least 85% of the screen. That budget is what stops the UI creeping back.
  • Controls live in the corner arcs, roughly 60–80° from where the thumbs sit on a two-handed grip. The centre is the "ow zone", marked here in red: reaching it means letting go of the tablet.
  • The timeline is bottom-only, full width, with an arrow button at each end. Swipe is offered as well, never instead — swipe fails with gloves and fails one-handed.
  • Every control is at least a 44 pt touch target even though the strips are thin. Thin strip, big targets: those are not in conflict.
  • The strips are opaque paper with a real border, because they sit over a live camera feed.

Open questions

  • Two corner strips plus a timeline is three things at the bottom edge. Does the timeline want its own state, shown only while scrubbing?
  • Should the left strip mirror for a left-handed grip, or is the layout symmetrical enough already?
