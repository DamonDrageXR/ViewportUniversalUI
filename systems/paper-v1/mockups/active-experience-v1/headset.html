Wrist menu — headset

Target: Headset · comfortable FOV · 1280×800 · State 3 of 3 · Status: review · Updated 2026-09-17

Intent

The running experience in a headset, where the rule that the view is sacred bites hardest: there is no screen edge to tuck a strip against, and anything placed in front of the wearer is placed in front of the work. So the interface goes on the body or on the object instead, and the space in front stays empty.

Why it is built this way

  • Three placements, none of them head-locked: a wrist menu read by turning the forearm over, a strip at keyboard height reached by dropping the hands, and a menu stuck to the object being worked on.
  • The wrist menu may sit outside the comfort band precisely because it is only visible when the arm is raised and turned — it is summoned, not displayed.
  • The keyboard-height strip sits below the eye line and curves to the wearer, because a flat strip at arm's length is further away at its ends than in its middle.
  • The object-stuck menu removes the question of what a control applies to: it is attached to its subject, with a leader line to prove it.
  • Everything reached by a ray or gaze is --hit-xr. The wrist menu is the exception at --hit-comfortable, because it is touched at 30 cm rather than pointed at across the room.

Open questions

  • Do the wrist menu and the keyboard strip both need to exist, or is one of them the fallback for the other?
  • Should the object menu follow the object if the wearer walks around it, or stay on the face it was opened from?
